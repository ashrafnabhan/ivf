# Knowledge of Evidence-Based and Low-Value Practices in Reproductive Endocrinology and Infertility Among Gynecologists: A Cross-Sectional Study

**Background:** Despite accumulating evidence on the effective of specific interventions in reproductive endocrinology and infertility (REI) care, low-value practices with limited or refuted benefit continue to be used, while some evidence-based practices remain underutilized. Physician knowledge is a key determinant of practice patterns, yet data on gynecologists' familiarity with current evidence in this area are limited, particularly in Egypt.

**Objective:** To assess gynecologists' knowledge of evidence-based and low-value REI practices, and to identify factors associated with adequate knowledge.

**Methods:** A cross-sectional survey was distributed to 210 eligible gynecologists in Egypt across healthcare facilities whether university, ministry of health, or private settings, of whom 169 responded (response rate 80.5%). A self-administered annonymous questionnaire assessed knowledge across 15 REI practices, generating a composite knowledge score (range 0–15). Adequate knowledge was defined as a score in the upper two quartiles of the distribution. Associations between adequate knowledge and respondent characteristics were assessed using chi-square and Kruskal-Wallis tests, followed by multivariable logistic and linear regression.

**Results:** The median knowledge score was 9 (IQR 5–12); 72 respondents (43%) demonstrated adequate knowledge. Knowledge was highest for progesterone use in the luteal phase (94% correct) and lowest for corticosteroid use (20% correct) and low-molecular-weight heparin for recurrent implantation failure (26% correct). On multivariable analysis, provision of fertility-related care was the only independent predictor of both adequate knowledge (OR 3.06, 95% CI 1.11–9.90, p = .041, for non-providers vs providers) and total knowledge score (β = −3.0, 95% CI −4.5 to −1.4, p \< .001). Sex, degree, subspecialty status, age, and years of practice were not independently associated with knowledge after adjustment.

**Conclusion:** Knowledge of evidence-based and low-value REI practices is limited among gynecologists, particularly those not actively providing fertility-related care. Targeted educational interventions for non-REI-focused practitioners may help reduce the use of low-value practices and support evidence-based decision-making.
