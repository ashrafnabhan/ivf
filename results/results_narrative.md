# Results

## Descriptive statistics of continuous variables

Data were available for age in 167 respondents (2 missing), years of postgraduate practice in 160 respondents (9 missing), and knowledge score in all 169 respondents. The mean age was 40.2 years (SD 10.7; median 36.0, IQR 32.0–46.0; range 29.0–70.0), and the mean duration of postgraduate practice was 16.4 years (SD 10.6; median 12.0, IQR 8.0–22.0; range 6.0–46.0). The mean total knowledge score was 8.37 out of 15 (SD 3.81; median 9, IQR 5.0–12.0; range 0–15). Shapiro-Wilk testing indicated that all three variables departed significantly from a normal distribution (p < .001 for age, years of practice, and knowledge score), so non-parametric statistics and tests were used for subsequent analyses involving these variables.

## Baseline characteristics of the study sample

A total of 169 respondents were included in the analysis. The median age was 36 years (IQR 32–46) and the median duration of postgraduate practice was 12 years (IQR 8–22). Of the respondents, 90 (53%) were male and 79 (47%) were female. Ninety-seven respondents (57%) held an MD or MRCOG, compared with 72 (43%) who held a Master's degree or diploma. Forty-two respondents (25%) reported a subspecialty in reproductive endocrinology and infertility, while the remaining 127 (75%) did not. The large majority, 142 respondents (84%), reported providing fertility-related care, whereas 27 (16%) did not. Among those who provided such care, duration of provision was fairly evenly distributed: 52 (37%) for less than 5 years, 42 (30%) for five to ten years, and 48 (34%) for more than 10 years. Regarding practice setting, 102 respondents (60%) worked in a multi-setting practice, 48 (28%) in the public sector only, and 19 (11%) in private practice only. Most respondents, 106 (63%), reported having access to relevant clinical guidelines, while 63 (37%) did not.

## Knowledge of individual practices

Correct-response rates varied considerably across the 15 knowledge items assessed. Awareness was highest for progesterone use in the luteal phase (159 respondents, 94% correct) and GnRH antagonist protocols (143, 85% correct), followed by endometrial scratching (122, 72%), preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy (117, 69%), ERT testing (114, 67%), sperm DNA fragmentation index testing (110, 65%), and hysteroscopy prior to IVF (103, 61%). Knowledge was more limited for intravenous immunoglobulin use (101, 60%), assisted hatching (90, 53%), LIF therapy (82, 49%), lymphocyte immunization therapy (72, 43%), and intralipid infusion (73, 43%). The lowest correct-response rates were observed for elective freeze-all strategies (51, 30%), low-molecular-weight heparin for recurrent implantation failure (44, 26%), and corticosteroid use (33, 20%). Based on the composite score, 72 respondents (43%) demonstrated adequate overall knowledge, while 97 (57%) were classified as having inadequate knowledge; the median total knowledge score was 9 (IQR 5–12).

## Association between knowledge adequacy and respondent characteristics

Knowledge adequacy differed significantly by sex, with men more likely than women to demonstrate adequate knowledge (46/90, 51.1% vs 26/79, 32.9%; p = .017). Respondents holding an MD or MRCOG were also more likely to have adequate knowledge (49/97, 50.5%) than those with a Master's degree or diploma (23/72, 31.9%) (p = .016). Adequate knowledge was more common among respondents who provided fertility-related care (67/142, 47.2%) than among those who did not (5/27, 18.5%) (p = .006), and it increased with longer duration of care provision, from 18/52 (34.6%) among those providing care for under 5 years, to 20/42 (47.6%) for 5–10 years, to 29/48 (60.4%) for more than 10 years (p = .036). A trend toward higher knowledge adequacy was observed among respondents with a subspecialty in reproductive endocrinology and infertility (23/42, 54.8%) compared with those without (49/127, 38.6%), although this did not reach statistical significance (p = .066). Similarly, differences by practice setting (multi-setting 49/102, 48.0%; private 9/19, 47.4%; public 14/48, 29.2%; p = .084) and by guideline access (access 42/106, 39.6%; no access 30/63, 47.6%; p = .3) were not statistically significant.

## Association between total knowledge score and respondent characteristics

Consistent with the findings above, the total knowledge score itself varied significantly by several characteristics. Respondents with an MD or MRCOG scored higher (median 10, IQR 7–12) than those with a Master's degree or diploma (median 7, IQR 5–10; p < .001). Respondents who provided fertility-related care scored substantially higher (median 9, IQR 7–12) than those who did not (median 4, IQR 2–8; p < .001), and scores rose progressively with longer duration of provision, from a median of 8 (IQR 5–11) for under 5 years, to 9 (IQR 8–11) for 5–10 years, to 10 (IQR 8–12) for more than 10 years (p = .040). Respondents with a subspecialty in reproductive endocrinology and infertility also scored higher (median 10, IQR 8–12) than those without (median 8, IQR 5–11; p = .035). Scores did not differ significantly by practice setting (multi-setting median 9, IQR 7–12; private median 6, IQR 4–12; public median 8, IQR 4–10; p = .056) or by guideline access (access median 9, IQR 5–12; no access median 9, IQR 6–11; p = .6).

## Multivariable logistic regression of adequate knowledge

On multivariable logistic regression adjusting for age, years of postgraduate practice, sex, degree, subspecialty in reproductive endocrinology and infertility, and provision of fertility-related care, the only independent predictor was provision of care itself: respondents who did not provide such care had roughly three times higher odds of inadequate knowledge relative to those who did (OR 3.06, 95% CI 1.11–9.90, p = .041). No other variable reached statistical significance after adjustment, including age (OR 0.92, 95% CI 0.74–1.12, p = .4), years of practice (OR 1.06, 95% CI 0.87–1.32, p = .6), sex (male vs female: OR 0.56, 95% CI 0.28–1.13, p = .11), degree (OR 1.41, 95% CI 0.66–3.01, p = .4), and subspecialty status (OR 1.70, 95% CI 0.76–3.86, p = .2).

## Multivariable linear regression of total knowledge score

A parallel multivariable linear regression using the continuous knowledge score as the outcome confirmed provision of fertility-related care as the sole independent predictor: respondents who did not provide such care scored on average 3.0 points lower than those who did (coefficient −3.0, 95% CI −4.5 to −1.4, p < .001), after adjustment for age, years of practice, sex, degree, and subspecialty status. None of the remaining covariates were independently associated with knowledge score: age (coefficient 0.19, 95% CI −0.15 to 0.52, p = .3), years of practice (coefficient −0.19, 95% CI −0.54 to 0.15, p = .3), sex (coefficient 0.59, 95% CI −0.58 to 1.8, p = .3), degree (coefficient −1.2, 95% CI −2.4 to 0.09, p = .068), and subspecialty status (coefficient −0.99, 95% CI −2.4 to 0.39, p = .2).
